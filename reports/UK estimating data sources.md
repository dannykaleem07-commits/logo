# License the data, encode the judgement

**The estimator cannot legally become a complete, all-makes OEM parts-and-labour database by compiling, scraping or copying, and today it does not hold one.** Its 212-part generic catalogue, its own guide times and 111 researched OEM part numbers for 8 vehicles are a starting point. They are not manufacturer catalogues or manufacturer labour times. Every route to the full datasets runs through a commercial licence. VIN-level OE part numbers and UK prices come from aggregators such as Infomedia and Partly, or from bespoke agreements with each manufacturer. Recognised labour and paint times come from partnering with Audatex, GT Motive or J.D. Power Repair Estimate, or from bespoke Thatcham, AZT and OEM contracts. Exact derivative identification needs a DVLA-licensed commercial lookup such as cap hpi, and pre-accident values need licences for the guides the Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) names: CAP, Glass's, Autotrader and Percayso. A large part of what makes software think like an engineer needs no licence, though. That includes the free DVLA and DVSA government APIs, the PD 35 report structure, the ABI salvage-category logic, the AZT-style paint-stage *structure*, ADAS calibration prompts, and an AI valuation assistant that works from guide figures and adverts the engineer types in. That is where to build first. **One caveat applies to the whole report.** The research team could read only search-result snippets and summaries, because direct fetches of every primary page were blocked by DNS. That included partslink24's terms, thatcham.org, audatex.co.uk, the DVLA and DVSA developer portals, the FCA and FOS sites, legislation.gov.uk, the ABI Code PDF and the National Archives. Exact wording, prices and field names below must be checked against the live pages before they go into code, contracts or report templates.

## Manufacturer parts data is licensed per user, not sold as a feed

No source turned up a public API covering all makes that returns VIN-level OE part numbers and UK list prices *and* grants redistribution rights. The routes that exist fall into three tiers. **Infomedia** (owner of Microcat) offers a VIN API that resolves a VIN, or a plate plus country, to a vehicle. Its Parts API then returns the correct parts with supersessions, quantities and country-based pricing. Both are approval-gated and aimed at OEMs, dealers and authorised partners ([Infomedia Parts API](https://www.infomedia.com.au/apis/parts-api/); [Infomedia VIN API](https://www.infomedia.com.au/apis/vin-api/)). **Partly** says it licenses OEM data directly from manufacturers and already runs a UK Collision Repair Network that recommends parts from the VIN plus collision area and severity ([Partly – How it works](https://partly.com/how-it-works); [Partly UKCRN](https://www.partly.com/sales-channels/ukcrn)). Neither publishes UK brand coverage, and neither confirmed Mercedes-Benz UK prices. The second tier is the incumbents. Audatex's AudaConnect releases data only "after Audatex grants permission" ([Audatex AudaConnect](https://audatex.co.uk/solutions/audaconnect)), and DAT runs a developer interface programme ([DAT Interface Partnership](https://www.datgroup.com/products/interface-partnership)). These are integration channels for their own customers, not wholesale data sales to a competing estimator. The third tier is the manufacturers' own portals: Mercedes-Benz B2B Connect, VW erWin, BMW AOS/ETK, Ford Microcat Market and Stellantis Service Box, plus LexCom's partslink24. These are per-user subscriptions for the subscriber's own use. B2B Connect shows prices "specific to your business" ([B2B Connect UK](https://b2bconnect.mercedes-benz.com/gb/campaign/uk-home)). TecDoc, the aftermarket standard, carries OE numbers only as cross-references against aftermarket articles ([Prodexa – TecDoc](https://prodexa.com/en/kb/tecdoc/)).

Repair-and-maintenance-information (RMI) law gives a right of *access*, not a right to republish. Annex X 6.1 of Regulation 2018/858, retained in GB law, requires a database "easily accessible to independent operators". The database must identify parts by VIN "and any additional criteria such as ... trim level or options", with OE numbers, OE names and validity dates ([legislation.gov.uk Annex X](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2018/858/annex/X/2022-12-31/data.htm), per search summary). The CJEU in *Scania* (C-319/22, 2023) held this must be machine-readable ([BAILII](https://www.bailii.org/eu/cases/EUECJ/2023/C31922.html)). Manufacturers may still charge "reasonable and proportionate" fees under Art. 63 ([legislation.gov.uk Art. 63](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2018/858/article/63/2020-01-31)), and list prices do not appear among the required fields in the excerpts found. Later EU amendments do not apply in GB ([Pinsent Masons](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/eu-vehicle-law-divergence-great-britain-northern-ireland)). SI 2022/1273 also struck "vehicle repair and maintenance information" from some provisions, with an unclear effect on parts data ([SI 2022/1273](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/1273/part/4/made?view=plain)), so specialist GB advice is needed. The UK Motor Vehicle Block Exemption Order 2023 is competition law. It penalises manufacturers who restrict independents, but it gives a software house no data right of its own ([Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2023/automotive-industry-a-new-motor-vehicles-competition-law-block-exemption-is-now-in-force/)).

Scraping is the trap. Under the UK database right (CRDR 1997 reg 16), extracting a substantial part of a database is an infringement, and so is the repeated, systematic extraction of insubstantial parts ([legislation.gov.uk reg 16](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/16); [5RB on BHB v William Hill](https://www.5rb.com/?p=5151)). *Ryanair v PR Aviation* lets a site ban scraping by contract even where the database right does not apply ([Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/ryanair-ltd-v-pr-aviation-bv-contracts-rights-and-users-in-a-low-cost-database-law/)). 7zap, a UK-governed mirror, expressly bans automated extraction, offers no API or bulk rights on paid plans, and disclaims any manufacturer affiliation, so the provenance of its own data is unknown ([7zap ToS](https://7zap.com/en/pages/terms-of-service/)). An estimator needs all makes, all VINs and continuous updates, and harvesting that from any EPC mirror or OEM portal is a substantial, systematic extraction by definition. **That is why the app's catalogue cannot simply be "filled in".** The data exists, but it is owned, and the owners license it per seat or per negotiated contract. Published prices are almost absent. The only UK figures found are old, undated Ford ones: Ecat at £7 and Microcat Market at £8 per user per month ([Ford UK](https://www.ford.co.uk/experience-ford/news/independent-operators); [Infomedia FordMarket order form](https://www.infomedia.com.au/files/training/market/ford/FordMarket-Order-Form-United-Kingdom_EN.pdf)).

The Mercedes-Benz E220d AMG Line Premium Plus 9G-Tronic shows why derivative-level accuracy depends on licensed build data. A W213 E 220 d is model code 213.004 ([B-Parts UK](https://www.b-parts.co.uk/auto-parts/vehicle/mercedes-benz-e-class-w213-e-220-d-213004-2016-996915)). But "AMG Line" and "Premium Plus" are option packages held on the VIN-linked datacard (SA codes), and the AMG Line bumper differs from the standard one. Only Mercedes' own systems or an OEM-licensed aggregator can read that datacard and return the right part, its supersession and its UK price. B2B Connect does exactly this when it "builds vehicle data from the VIN" ([B2B Connect UK terms](https://b2bconnect.mercedes-benz.com/gb/legal/terms-of-use)). Mercedes also markets an RMI licence that it says permits enrichment and redistribution (SaaS/DaaS) to eligible businesses, including damage assessors, in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK ([Mercedes-Benz RMI](https://drivesomethinggreater.com/products/Repair-Maintenance-Information)). That is the most promising single-OEM lead found.

## Recognised labour and paint times live inside three estimating systems

UK insurers recognise times produced by Audatex, GT Motive and J.D. Power's Repair Estimate. Repair Estimate replaced Glassmatix in 2017 and draws on data "contractually provided by over 40 manufacturers" ([Glass's Repair Estimate](https://glass.co.uk/repair-estimate-product-support/)). Audatex takes times directly from manufacturers. It uses Thatcham panel times for some marques, independent bodies where an OEM publishes none, and its own additions to fill gaps ([Audatex Australia](https://audatex.com.au/?p=154), the best available proxy for UK practice). In 2013, nine of the ten largest insurers recommended or required Audatex for their networks ([CC working paper 2013](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5329ded840f0b60a760002b2/130729_toh_3_horizontal_concentration_in_repair_cost_estimation_systems.pdf)). **Thatcham** is the industry's research body, but its live product, escribe, is a library of repair *methods*, not a times feed ([Thatcham escribe release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/thatcham-research/pressreleases/improved-online-repair-methods-to-meet-the-needs-of-the-crash-repair-sector-1664294)). Its historic Methods and Times data reached software only through bespoke deals: with systems houses, with Suncorp (£2m over five years in Australia and New Zealand) and with Mitchell ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news/australian-automotive-industry-draws-on-uk-repair-expertise-in-deal-with-thatcham-research/)). No developer API, licence terms or current price was found for Thatcham times or paint data.

Manufacturer labour times are reachable per VIN, not in bulk. Since April 2022 independents can buy access to Mercedes XENTRY Operation Time, which gives VIN-filtered operation times on credits plus per-user VIN activation ([B2B Connect XENTRY Combo](https://b2bconnect.mercedes-benz.com/gb/help/faq/software/xentry-combo-package)). VW's erWin sells hourly, daily or flat-rate access to repair information ([Volkswagen UK](https://www.volkswagen.co.uk/en/need-help/partnering-with-volkswagen/technical-form.html)). An engineer can lawfully look up an OEM time on the portal and type it into an estimate. A developer cannot bulk-load portal data into a product without a separate data licence from each of 30-plus manufacturers, and that set of contracts is exactly what the incumbents hold. Autodata and HaynesPro cover mechanical work, not panel, repair-level or refinish operations ([HaynesPro](https://www.haynespro.com/workshops)). ALLDATA EU Labour Times, built on TecAlliance data, claims body labour times and is worth a direct enquiry ([ALLDATA EU Labour Times](https://www.alldata.com/eu/en/alldata-labour-times)).

Paint follows **AZT**, the manufacturer-independent calculation used in more than 35 countries and built into Audatex, DAT, GT Motive and others ([AZT](https://www.azt-automotive.com/en/products-services/AZT-paint-calculation-system)). Its stages are the structure the app is missing. For metal they are surface, repair under 50%, repair over 50% and new part. For plastic they are surface, repair under 15%, new part prepared and new part unprepared, with "K"-coded stages for new plastic parts by delivery condition ([DAT LIF 00-060](https://www.dat.de/sphinx/help/printInfos/pdf/de/LIF_00-060_DE_EN.pdf); [AZT system description](https://www.azt-automotive.com/_Resources/Persistent/b70048078929e9cc459f872d1f3bcab6a62cf07d/AZT%20Paint%20Calculation_System%20Description_2020-10-27.pdf)). Audatex's own plastic new-part painting asks for the part's delivery condition (LE, LE2, LE3, LE4), which changes labour and materials ([Audatex AEG 4.0](https://audatex.co.uk/audaenterprisegold-4-0-feature-overview-plastic-new-part-painting￼/)). Materials are *calculated* from area, paint type and stage, not charged at a flat hourly rate ([Audatex UK paint update](https://audatex.co.uk/uk-paint-update-28th-february-2023/)). No published UK blend percentage was found. US systems once used 50% of refinish time for a two-stage blend. An SCRS study found blending took 31.59% *longer* than a full refinish, and vendors now say blend must be assessed vehicle by vehicle ([Collision Week](https://collisionweek.com/2022/11/02/scrs-study-concludes-time-to-blend-into-an-adjacent-panel-is-30-higher-than-full-panel-refinish-time/); [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2024/01/22/mitchell-profile-setting-changes-to-allow-for-user-defined-blend-percentages-wont-change-guidance/)). FOS accepts blending into adjacent panels as a fair repair ([FOS DRN-5913560](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5913560.pdf)). The app can therefore lawfully add blend and paint-stage *options*, with the engineer entering the hours. Reproducing AZT's *values* without a licence is not lawful, and cloning the stage structure needs a light legal check.

## Exact derivative needs a commercial lookup; the free APIs supply the cross-checks

The **DVLA Vehicle Enquiry Service (VES)** is free once registered and is keyed on the registration only. It returns make, colour, fuel, cc, CO2, tax and MOT status, month of first registration, wheelplan, type approval, export marker and date of last V5C ([DVLA VES spec](https://developer-portal.driver-vehicle-licensing.api.gov.uk/apis/vehicle-enquiry-service/v1.2.0-vehicle-enquiry-service.html)). It returns no VIN and, by most accounts, no model ([lifefile](https://lifefile-docs.pages.dev/developers/vehicle-lookup-setup/)). The new **DVSA MOT History API** replaced the legacy API on 1 September 2025. It uses OAuth2 plus an API key, registration takes up to five working days, and it returns make, a coarse model, fuel, engine size, key dates and every odometer reading ([DVSA docs](https://documentation.history.mot.api.gov.uk/); [register](https://documentation.history.mot.api.gov.uk/mot-history-api/register)). The DfT catalogue lists it as free under the Open Government Licence ([DfT](https://findtransportdata.dft.gov.uk/dataset/mot-history-api)). Together the two APIs give a free identity sanity check and a mileage history, which valuation and clocking checks need. Neither gives derivative or VIN, and cars under three years old have no MOT record.

Derivative, full VIN, engine code and industry keys come only from DVLA-licensed commercial providers. **cap hpi** links registrations to CAP ID, ABI, Glass's, MVRIS and TecDoc K-type, offers more than 150 data points, and claims to be the only TecDoc-approved UK registration lookup that includes K-type ([cap hpi VRM lookup](https://www.cap-hpi.com/?p=760); [SoftwareOne](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/cap-vehicle-identity-check/PCP-2932-4570)). Cheaper resellers quote by the call: Check Car Details charges £0.02 to £0.10 for vehicle data and £1.82 for a history check ([Check Car Details API](https://api.checkcardetails.co.uk)), and UK Vehicle Data returns `Derivative` and SMMT `ModelVariant` fields ([UKVD](https://ukvehicledata.co.uk/dvla-data-api)). Where their derivative strings originate was not established. Write-off markers come only from **HPI and Experian**, the two firms with MIAFTR access, so every reseller's write-off flag traces back to them ([Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-news/106336/exclusive-the-great-british-car-insurance-write-off-scandal-insurers-work-with-dvla)). Free VIN decoders are no substitute. NHTSA vPIC is built from US filings and often returns error code 8 on European VINs ([Carwhere](https://www.carwhere.com/guides/nhtsa-vin-decoder-api)). The VIN also does not encode options, so trim-level truth for the E220d example sits in OEM build data. Registrations stored against a claim are personal data under ICO guidance ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/what-is-personal-data/can-we-identify-an-individual-indirectly/)), and some providers' terms forbid caching results ([UKVD terms](https://ukvehicledata.co.uk/policies/WebsiteTerms)).

## Valuation evidence must start from the highest retail guide

FOS names its starting-point guides as "CAP, Glass's, Autotrader (the valuation guide, not sales adverts) and Percayso" ([FOS business guidance](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/businesses/resolving-complaint/complaints-deal/insurance/motor-insurance/vehicle-valuations-write-offs)). Market value is what the car "would have sold for at a reputable dealership just before it was damaged" ([FOS technical note](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/publications/technical_notes/motor-valuation.html)). The settlement should start from "the highest valuation returned by the trade guides". Averaging has been overturned ([FOS DRN-6207635](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-6207635.pdf)), and trade figures are rejected where retail is higher ([FOS DRN-5655696](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5655696.pdf)). The guides really do diverge: CAP gave £13,350, Glass's £14,840 and Cazana £15,411 for one car ([FOS DRN-3788739](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-3788739.pdf)). Adverts can justify a departure, or stand in for an old car the guides cannot value, but only if they are like-for-like ([FOS DRN-4750573](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4750573.pdf)). An engineer's valuation is not persuasive where real expertise is not evident ([FOS DRN-4719022](https://financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4719022.pdf)). The FCA's 2024 multi-firm review condemned flat 20% previous-write-off deductions and lowball first offers. It also extended scrutiny to "all insurers and third parties involved in valuing vehicles", which includes outsourced engineers ([FCA review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/findings-multi-firm-review-insurers-valuation-vehicles)). Redress reached about **£200m for about 270,000 motorists** ([FCA press release](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/over-270000-motorists-receive-motor-insurance-compensation-insurers)). A deduction for a previous Cat S or N marker needs evidence about that specific vehicle ([FOS FAQ](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/faqs/all/sort-complaints-car-write-offs-valuations)), and a private plate does not change the value ([FOS DRN-4107118](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4107118.pdf)). Courts apply the replacement-value measure from *Darbishire v Warran* ([Stokes v McAuley](https://www.judiciaryni.uk/files/judiciaryni/decisions/Stokes%20%28Gemma%29%20v%20McAuley%20%28Robert%20James%29.pdf)) and scrutinise unexplained engineer PAVs ([Weightmans](https://www.weightmans.com/media-centre/news/when-expert-engineering-evidence-in-a-vehicle-damage-and-credit-hire-claim-is-not-what-it-seems/)).

That gives the AI valuation assistant a clear evidence standard. Its input is each guide's *retail* figure at the loss date, with the guide's name, date, mileage, condition grade and spec recorded. Its default is the highest guide figure, and any lower figure needs a reason backed by like-for-like adverts. Mileage comes from the MOT history, and deductions are allowed only from comparables for that specific vehicle. Its output is a workings table and a range of opinion, not a bare number. Instant-buy offers and part-exchange figures are shown as context only. The best-documented licensed source is **Autotrader Connect**. It returns trade, private, retail and part-exchange values across five condition grades, and its Trended Valuations give historic values by date, which suits retrospective reports ([Autotrader Current Valuations](https://help.autotrader.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/21923133513117-Introduction-to-Current-Valuations); [Trended](https://help.autotrader.co.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/21946125008029-Introduction-to-Trended-Valuations)). The only unit price found is **£0.40 per Autotrader value via MotorCheck**, which also resells Glass's and Percayso ([MotorCheck](https://help.motorcheck.co.uk/en/articles/4730661-auto-trader-valuations)). Whether Autotrader will license Connect directly to an engineering firm was not confirmed. Salvage should come from real auction results or bids, not a fixed percentage. No published UK return rates by category were found, and the 20–40% figures in circulation come from low-reliability blogs ([car.co.uk](https://www.car.co.uk/media/blogs/scrapping-your-car/what-is-the-salvage-value-of-my-car-expert-insights)).

## Professional standards translate directly into enforceable software rules

**The ABI Salvage Code V12** (dated March 2025, published 28 May 2025) keeps four categories. Cat A is scrap. Cat B means the shell is crushed and listed parts may be salvaged. Cat S is repairable with damage to the structural frame or chassis. Cat N is repairable with no structural damage. V12 adds a structural-battery test and an EV/HV matrix in Section 8, and a dispute process led by an Appropriately Qualified Person (AQP) ([ABI news](https://www.abi.org.uk/news/news-articles/2025/5/abi-continues-focus-on-road-safety-with-updates-to-its-salvage-code-of-practice/); [ABI Code PDF](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2025/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf); [WriteOffCars](https://www.writeoffcars.co.uk/v11-v12-comparison/)). Only an AQP holding an IAEA-issued unique identifier may categorise salvage, and "AQP" must not be used as a post-nominal ([IAEA Salvage Code exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam/the-salvage-code-exam)). Sources conflict on whether a damaged structural battery means Cat S or Cat A/B, so the app must route EVs through the matrix text rather than guess ([DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/8/insurers-calls-for-more-regulation-on-electric-vehicles)). In software terms: any damaged structural component blocks Cat N; a category needs the engineer's AQP identifier; Cat B needs a list of salvageable parts; and EVs prompt for HV-battery structure, damage and labelling. The code sets no economic threshold. Insurer triggers of about 50–70% of PAV are commercial choices and should be a configurable setting ([Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/whats-it-worth-to-you/1376699.article); [FOS DRN-5763454](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5763454.pdf)).

**The IAEA and IMI** define who signs the report. IAEA grades run from Affiliate, which implies no competence, through Associate (A.Inst.A.E.A.) to Member (M.Inst.A.E.A.) ([IAEA grades](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/membership-grades)). IMI Vehicle Damage Assessor accreditations expire ([IMI register entry](https://www.theimi.org.uk/member-register/person/20210100750)), so the app should warn when an accreditation has lapsed by the report date. The IAEA practical exam fails any specification that leaves out a structural or SRS part ([IAEA practical exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/education/practical-examination)), which gives the app a ready-made validation rule. **CPR Part 35 and PD 35** fix the contents of the report. It must set out qualifications, literature relied on, material instructions, which facts are within the engineer's own knowledge, who examined the vehicle, any range of opinion, a summary of conclusions, the duty declaration, and the prescribed statement of truth with the contempt sentence ([PD 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35/pd_part35)). On the small claims track, expert evidence needs permission and fees are capped at £750. Written questions must come within 28 days and be for clarification only ([Keoghs](https://keoghs.co.uk/keoghs-insight/expert-evidence-small-claims); [Stewarts](https://www.stewartslaw.com/?p=10554)). The **Civil Justice Council's final report of 29 October 2026** proposes a declaration where AI "generates or influences the substantive content" of an expert report ([CJC final report](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Use-of-AI-for-the-Preparation-of-Court-Documents-Final-Report.pdf)). The app's Claude-based photo labelling and narrative drafting therefore need an AI-use log and an optional declaration now. Reports fail in court when the notes, photos and report do not match, as in *Hodroje* ([Weightmans](https://www.weightmans.com/media-centre/news/when-expert-engineering-evidence-in-a-vehicle-damage-and-credit-hire-claim-is-not-what-it-seems/)), and when the expert's independence is compromised, as in *Wiltshire v Aioi Nissay Dowa* ([Deka Chambers](https://www.dekachambers.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Civil-Fraud-Credit-Update-slide-16.10.2025-2.pdf)). The app should therefore capture the inspection type, a photo-coverage checklist, photo metadata and a conflicts declaration.

**BS 10125:2022** underpins the BSI Kitemark for vehicle damage repair ([BSI](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-IE/products-and-services/assessment-and-certification/product-testing-certification/deliver-safe-and-compliant-vehicle-repairs-to-bs-10125/)), and insurers expect S and N repairs to follow OEM or Thatcham methods. For **ADAS**, Thatcham's 2020 Insurance Industry Requirements say inspection, realignment and calibration "must be considered" whenever sensors, nearby parts or geometry are touched, with verifiable evidence of calibration to OEM tolerances ([Thatcham IIR Guidance](https://www.thatcham.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/006-IIR-Guidance-July-2020.pdf)). No recognised UK calibration *times* exist, and no "PAS 1886" ADAS standard could be identified.

## Prioritised integration plan

Priority 1 can ship now with no licence. Priority 2 uses low-cost commercial APIs. Priority 3 covers the enterprise licences needed for complete parts and recognised times. Where the table says "not published", the research found no price, and none has been estimated.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that "the brains of an engineer" and "the data of an estimating house" are different assets with different legal status. The brains, meaning the categorisation rules, the Part 35 discipline, ADAS triggers, paint-stage logic and the FOS valuation hierarchy, are public, and the app can encode them now, which makes it more defensible than many licensed systems in exactly the places where reports fail in court. The data is private property held under decades of contracts. The app's honest position is therefore a hybrid: a rules engine that is strong without any licence, plus pluggable slots for licensed identity, valuation, parts and times data, with every number tagged by source and date. The likely first commercial unlock is cheap. A derivative-level registration lookup plus one FOS-named guide via a reseller costs pence to pounds per report. Complete OEM parts and recognised times are a partnership decision rather than an engineering task, and the open questions to settle with vendors and lawyers are Infomedia's and Partly's UK brand coverage, Mercedes' RMI redistribution terms, and whether GB retained law still carries the full Annex X duty.
