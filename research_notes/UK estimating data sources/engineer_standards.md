## 1. ABI Code of Practice for the Categorisation of Motorised Vehicle Salvage (V12, 2025)

### Takeaway
The current code is V12 (dated March 2025, published 28 May 2025). It replaced V11 (Nov 2019), keeps the A/B/S/N categories, adds the first EV/hybrid rules (including a "structural battery" test and a separate HV-battery matrix), clarifies disposal of body structures/chassis, and formalises AQP-led disputes. Only an IAEA-assessed "Appropriately Qualified Person" (AQP) may categorise salvage. The code is voluntary, not statutory.

### Cited Findings
- Current version is V12, dated March 2025, published 28 May 2025; previous was V11 (November 2019). — [WriteOffCars status page](https://www.writeoffcars.co.uk/v12-current-status/); official PDF: [ABI Code PDF (2025 path)](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2025/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf) (also mirrored at [ABI keystone CMS, uploaded 2026/01](https://keystone-cms-production-amfyhhecavg8gneq.northeurope-01.azurewebsites.net/blob/keystoneproduction/publicFile/2026/01/code-practice-categorisation-motorised-vehicle-salvage-may2025-cmkdwbso4000dmho27coi4aly.pdf))
- ABI announcement (May 2025): update made as part of a focus on road safety; adds guidance for battery electric and hybrid vehicles, including safety labelling and controlled storage; clarifies disposal rules for body structures, chassis and frames; updates wording for HGVs and motorcycles. ABI still calls for the code to be put on a statutory footing. — [ABI news, May 2025](https://www.abi.org.uk/news/news-articles/2025/5/abi-continues-focus-on-road-safety-with-updates-to-its-salvage-code-of-practice/); [Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/electric-and-hybrid-vehicles-tackled-in-abi-salvage-code-update/1455345.article); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/auto-motor/abi-issues-revised-salvage-code-to-cover-evs-and-hybrids-537182.aspx); [Bodyshop mag](https://www.bodyshopmag.com/?p=51969)
- The code is voluntary, created because there was no regulation for written-off vehicles. — [ASM Autos summary](https://www.asm-autos.co.uk/blog/abi-vehicle-salvage-code-of-practice/)
- Four categories, which all motorised vehicle salvage must be given: A (scrap/recycle), B (break), S (repairable, structural), N (repairable, non-structural). — [ABI Code PDF](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2025/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf) via search summary
- Cat S definition: "a repairable vehicle which has sustained damage to any part of the structural frame or chassis" where the insurer/self-insured owner has decided not to repair it. — [Copart, Cat S](https://www.copart.co.uk/Content/UK/EN/Member-News/Category-S-Motor-Salvage)
- Cat N definition: "repairable vehicle which has not sustained damage to the structural frame or chassis and the insurer/self-insured owner has decided not to repair the vehicle." — [Copart, Introducing Cat N](https://www.copart.co.uk/Content/UK/EN/Member-News/Introducing-Category-N)
- Cat N can still involve significant damage to non-structural safety parts such as brakes, steering or airbags. — [Raw2K](https://www.raw2k.co.uk/news/all-your-questions-answered-about-category-n-salvage-cars)
- S and N replaced the old C and D from October 2017. — [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-news/consumer-news/99750/cat-a-b-s-and-n-cars-new-insurance-write-off-categories-from-october); [Copart 2017](https://www.copart.co.uk/Content/UK/EN/Member-News/Categories-replacement-S&N)
- Cat A: whole vehicle crushed; no parts may be salvaged or sold. — [AA](https://theaa.com/car-insurance/advice/damage-categories); [Autocar Cat B/A guide](https://www.autocar.co.uk/node/136130)
- Cat B: body shell/chassis must be scrapped; usable parts may be recovered and sold. One source says the salvageable parts are listed in the salvage report and that selling anything else is an offence. — [Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/node/136130); [AA](https://theaa.com/car-insurance/advice/damage-categories); [Scrap Local](https://scraplocal.co.uk/guides/a-guide-to-category-b-vehicles) (the "criminal offence" claim is from a low-authority source; treat with caution)
- The structural-area illustrations in the code are "generic and for guidance purposes only"; vehicle design and component descriptions differ by manufacturer. — [Copart Cat S page](https://www.copart.co.uk/Content/UK/EN/Member-News/Category-S-Motor-Salvage); [ABI PDF](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2019/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf)
- V12 asks whether the vehicle "sustained structural damage (including structural battery)". — [ABI PDF](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2019/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf) via search snippet
- EV rules: a Section 8 categorisation matrix for high-voltage battery EVs; the code separates structural from non-structural HV batteries; mega-castings appear in the structural list; assessors should be trained in HV systems and know OEM repair procedures. — [WriteOffCars V11 vs V12](https://www.writeoffcars.co.uk/v11-v12-comparison/); [WriteOffCars EV guide 2026](https://www.writeoffcars.co.uk/electric-vehicle-write-offs/)
- CONFLICT on the effect of structural battery damage: one commentary says a damaged structural battery makes the vehicle Cat S (so damage that is Cat N on an ICE car can be Cat S on an EV). Another says damage to a structural battery enclosure built into the chassis can mean Cat A/B. — [WriteOffCars](https://www.writeoffcars.co.uk/v11-v12-comparison/) vs [DWF, Aug 2025](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/8/insurers-calls-for-more-regulation-on-electric-vehicles) (attribution between these two sources is from a merged search summary; resolve against the Section 8 matrix in the PDF)
- AQP: the code requires salvage to be categorised only by an AQP. An AQP has a comprehensive technical education and training record plus a current competency assessment on salvage categorisation from the IAEA (or an equivalent recognised body), and is identified by a competency-based unique identifier. — [IAEA Salvage Code exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam/the-salvage-code-exam); [ABI PDF via search](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2025/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf)
- V12 adds a "clearer, more efficient" dispute process using AQPs. The code has a section on disputes between the insurer/self-insured and other interested parties. — [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/05/29/new-salvage-code-of-conduct-from-the-abi/); [ABI PDF](https://www.abi.org.uk/globalassets/files/publications/public/motor/2025/codepracticecategorisationmotorisedvehiclesalvagemay2025.pdf)
- IAEA moved AQP testing to a new V12 question bank after 31 July 2025. — [IAEA Salvage Code intro](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam)

### Inferences
- Software checks: (a) a category field restricted to A/B/S/N; (b) require the categorising engineer's AQP unique identifier whenever a category is assigned; (c) if any structural component is damaged (as the engineer marks it, with the OEM's structural definition taking priority over the generic illustrations), block Cat N and force S, A or B; (d) for BEV/PHEV/HEV, prompt "Is the HV battery structural? Is it damaged? Housing breached?" and send the case through the Section 8 matrix; (e) for Cat B, require a list of salvageable parts; (f) prompt for EV safety labelling and quarantine/storage notes.
- Cat S/N describe the damage type of a vehicle the insurer has chosen not to repair. The code itself does not set an economic threshold.
- The airbag question: search results did not show SRS deployment as a structural trigger in itself. Under N, airbag damage is cited as non-structural safety damage. The app should not treat deployment alone as Cat S without checking the code text.

### Gaps
- Could not read the V12 text: the exact structural component lists (Illustrations 1–3), the dispute-resolution steps and timescales, the motorcycle/HGV wording, and the Section 8 EV matrix. These need the PDF.
- Whether the categorisation must be recorded or notified within a set time (e.g., to MIAFTR or DVLA) was not confirmed in 2025-dated sources.

## 2. IAEA and IMI: qualifications, grades, codes of conduct, report standards

### Takeaway
The IAEA (Institute of Automotive Engineer Assessors, founded 1932, based in Milton Keynes) is the specialist professional body. Its grades are Affiliate (no competence implied), Associate (A.Inst.A.E.A.), Member (M.Inst.A.E.A.) and Fellowships. It runs the AQP salvage exam and a Code of Conduct. The IMI's equivalent is the time-limited Vehicle Damage Assessor (VDA) Accreditation plus the Professional Register. A Level 4 VDA apprenticeship standard (ST0406) also exists. Neither body appeared to publish a dedicated report-writing standard.

### Cited Findings
- The IAEA was founded in 1932; an undated article gives about 1,200 members worldwide. — [Bodyshop mag](https://www.bodyshopmag.com/?p=7520)
- IAEA grades: Affiliate "does not convey or confirm any form of qualification or level of competency"; Associate (A.Inst.A.E.A.) by oral examination or two IAEA written exams plus relevant training; Member (M.Inst.A.E.A.) needs at least four written exams (Sections 2, 3 and 4 mandatory) plus a practical exam under four years old (or an equivalent such as IMI VDA, with conditions), and the applicant must be working as an engineer assessor on motor damage claims. Generally at least two years' experience in a responsible position. Fellowships are by Council nomination. — [IAEA membership grades](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/membership-grades); [IAEA Member](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/membership-grades/full-membership); [IAEA Associate](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/membership-grades/associate); [IAEA About](https://www.iaea-online.org/about)
- AQP is not a recognised post-nominal and members must not use it as one. — [IAEA post-nominals](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/membership-grades/post-nominals); [IAEA Salvage Code exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam/the-salvage-code-exam)
- AQP exam: online, three sections each with its own pass mark: technical underpinning knowledge 70%, Code of Practice 80%, categorisation case studies 80%. Failed sections can be retaken alone within three months, otherwise all three. Fee £225 (affiliates/non-members) or £175 (Associates/Members). Passing candidates get an AQP Unique Identifier. Before starting, candidates must accept a Code of Conduct committing them to the Code of Practice; breach can mean losing the identifier. Desktop case studies give a vehicle description, a damage summary and a repair with 3–4 images, and the candidate allocates a category. — [IAEA exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam/the-salvage-code-exam); [IAEA assessment overview](https://www.iaea-online.org/salvage-code-exam/assessment-overview); [AQP syllabus PDF](https://www.iaea-online.org/media/content/aqp-syllabus.pdf)
- IAEA Code of Conduct covers duty of care and diligence, conflicts of interest, misrepresentation, and appropriate remuneration. A complaints process exists. — [IAEA About](https://www.iaea-online.org/about); [IAEA complaints](https://www.iaea-online.org/about/complaints)
- IAEA practical exam: the candidate checks a fully costed repair specification with photos (vehicle description, equipment/safety features, repair method and costs), finds the errors, and reports to principals what must change before acceptance. A specification that leaves out a key structural panel/part or an SRS component is unsafe, and including one is an automatic fail. — [IAEA practical exam](https://www.iaea-online.org/education/practical-examination)
- In 2018 Audatex trialled a new IAEA practical exam format with the IAEA. — [Audatex](https://audatex.co.uk/?p=11452)
- IMI VDA Accreditation: for people who estimate accident repairs. It certifies the ability to establish and record the circumstances of the damage, record the actual damage, and check the damage matches the circumstances. — [IMI Accident Repair Accreditation](https://tide.theimi.org.uk/learn/accreditations/accident-repair-accreditation); [IMI ACC-VDA-3-18](https://www.theimi.org.uk/learn/qualifications/ACC-VDA-3-18)
- IMI accreditations expire. One register entry shows a VDA Full Route accreditation expiring 17/11/26. — [IMI Professional Register entry](https://www.theimi.org.uk/member-register/person/20210100750)
- IMI Professional Register grades: FIMI, MIMI, AMIMI, CAE (3 yrs + Level 3 or IMI Accreditation), AAE, CAT. Members accept the IMI Code of Conduct. — [IMI professional register](https://www.theimi.org.uk/membership/professional-register); [IMI doc](https://www.theimi.org.uk/sites/default/files/documents/233332_2.pdf)
- Level 4 Vehicle Damage Assessor apprenticeship standard (ST0406), 36 months. — [IfATE](https://www.instituteforapprenticeships.org/apprenticeship-standards/vehicle-damage-assessor)
- AMVEA (Association of Motor Vehicle Engineers & Assessors) also has a members' code of conduct (secondary source). — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/CharlesHGGriffin/guide-lines-code-of-practice-for-the-individual-amvea1)

### Inferences
- Report header fields to capture and check: engineer name; IAEA grade and post-nominal (A.Inst.A.E.A. / M.Inst.A.E.A.; never "AQP" as a post-nominal); AQP unique identifier (needed if a salvage category is given); IMI accreditation number and expiry date (warn if expired at the report date); IMI register grade.
- Built-in checks that mirror the IAEA practical exam: every structural part and SRS component affected must appear in the repair specification; the vehicle description and equipment/ADAS features must be confirmed; the damage must be consistent with the stated circumstances.

### Gaps
- No IAEA or IMI published report-writing standard or template was found. No IAEA guidance on remote/image-based assessments was found (only the exam's use of images).
- Full text of the IAEA Code of Conduct was not retrieved.

## 3. CPR Part 35, PD 35, tracks, and pre-action protocols / GTA

### Takeaway
Once a report is used as expert evidence, CPR 35 and PD 35 set its mandatory contents: qualifications, literature, material instructions, facts vs opinion, who did any examination, range of opinion, summary of conclusions, any qualification, the duty declaration, and the prescribed statement of truth with the contempt warning. On the small claims track expert evidence needs permission (CPR 27.5) and fees are capped at £750. Written questions come within 28 days, for clarification only. Engineer reports are a recoverable disbursement in the RTA portal, but credit hire is outside the OIC small-claims protocol. The ABI/CHO GTA governs hire rates and periods between subscribers and now has binding arbitration for invoices under £10,000.

### Cited Findings
- PD 35 para 3.2: the report must give qualifications, literature relied on, the substance of all material facts and instructions, which facts are within the expert's own knowledge, any range of opinion (summarised, with reasons for the expert's view), a summary of conclusions, any qualification on the opinion, and a statement that the expert understands and has complied with the duty to the court. — [PD 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35/pd_part35)
- [verify] PD 35 para 3.2 also requires saying who carried out any examination, measurement or test the expert used, their qualifications, and whether it was done under the expert's supervision. The 3.2(9) declaration also confirms awareness of Part 35, PD 35 and the Guidance for the Instruction of Experts. — [PD 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35/pd_part35)
- PD 35 para 3.3 statement of truth (prescribed): "I confirm that I have made clear which facts and matters referred to in this report are within my own knowledge and which are not. Those that are within my own knowledge I confirm to be true. The opinions I have expressed represent my true and complete professional opinions on the matters to which they refer." Contempt wording added from 1 Oct 2020: "I understand that proceedings for contempt of court may be brought against anyone who makes, or causes to be made, a false statement in a document verified by a statement of truth without an honest belief in its truth." — [PD 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35/pd_part35); [122nd PD update](https://www.justice.gov.uk/documents/122-pd-update.pdf) [verify exact wording against the live text]
- CPR 35.10: the report must comply with PD 35 and end with the duty statement. CPR 35.3: the overriding duty is to the court. CPR 35.4: permission is needed, with the field, issues and estimated cost. CPR 35.5(2): no oral attendance unless needed in the interests of justice. — [CPR Part 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35); [DAC Beachcroft](https://www.dacbeachcroft.com/en/what-we-think/blackpool-borough-council-joint-testing-house-or-single-joint-expert)
- CPR 35.6: written questions must be proportionate, put once, within 28 days of service, for clarification only unless the court allows or the other side agrees. Answers form part of the report. Copy to the other side (PD 35 para 6.1). — [Stewarts](https://www.stewartslaw.com/?p=10554); [CPR 35](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part35)
- Small claims: CPR 27.5 says no expert evidence (written or oral) without permission. Usually one expert per issue. Expert fees capped at £750 (PD 27). Only evidence reasonably required is allowed. — [Keoghs](https://keoghs.co.uk/keoghs-insight/expert-evidence-small-claims); [CJC Guidance 2014](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/experts-guidance-cjc-aug-2014-amended-dec-8.pdf); [CPR 27 (2023)](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/3132/part/27/2023-10-01/data.html)
- A May 2026 MoJ consultation proposes amendments to CPR Part 27. Content was not reviewed. — [MoJ consultation PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69f9d47a903bc40650ede4dd/proposed-amendments-part-27-consultation.pdf)
- A study source says expert valuation evidence is usually unnecessary for ordinary second-hand car values because published guides exist (secondary study aid; weak authority). — [Brainscape](https://brainscape.com/flashcards/20-expert-evidence-17904530/packs/22274440)
- RTA protocols: the Low Value PI RTA protocol (from 31 July 2013) covers earlier/higher claims. The RTA Small Claims protocol (OIC) applies to accidents from 31 May 2021. Vehicle-related damages (PAV, repair, excess, hire) do not count toward the £25,000 limit under the older protocol. An engineer's report is a recoverable disbursement in protocol claims. In OIC, "non-protocol vehicle costs" (credit hire, subrogated claims) are excluded. — [RTA PAP 2013](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/protocol/prot_rta); [OIC guide v35, Apr 2024](https://www.officialinjuryclaim.org.uk/media/1447/guide-to-making-a-personal-injury-claim-version-35-april-2024.pdf); [Keoghs credit hire in portal](https://keoghs.co.uk/keoghs-insight/credit-hire-in-the-moj-portal); [SI 2013/1695](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1695/rule/7/made/data.pdf)
- GTA: a voluntary ABI–CHO protocol for daily rates, hire duration and ancillary charges. A revised "GTA 2.0" uses two independent data providers for rates. Binding arbitration for voluntary members applies to credit hire invoices under £10,000 still unresolved after 61 days. Commentary flags disputes over the seven-day period after a total loss, clause 4.14 and the booking-in of driveable vehicles. — [Insurance Business, GTA overhaul](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/auto-motor/motor-insurers-cho-collaborate-on-gta-overhaul-514869.aspx); [Verisk ADR](https://www.verisk.com/en-gb/blog/dispute-resolution-comes-to-the-credit-hire-space/); [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/blog/alternative-dispute-resolution-comes-to-credit-hire/print/); [Post, GTA tech](https://www.postonline.co.uk/claims/3536706/the-abi-credit-hire-gta-needs-to-better-reflect-todays-vehicle-technology)
- NEW (Oct 2026): the CJC final report on AI in preparing court documents (29 Oct 2026, 15 recommendations) proposes a new declaration and revised guidance for experts where AI "generates or influences the substantive content" of an expert's report. It rejects AI declarations otherwise. The 2014 CJC Guidance for the Instruction of Experts is being revised, with AI best practice to be added. CPR 35 has no AI-specific rule yet. — [CJC final report](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Use-of-AI-for-the-Preparation-of-Court-Documents-Final-Report.pdf); [Lewis Silkin, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/05/cjc-final-report-on-ai-and-court-documents-declarations-for-witnesses-and-expert-102o8fa); [Gowling](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/ai-in-court-cjc-issues-final-report-on-the-use-of-ai-in-preparing-court-documents); [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/ai-and-the-expectations-for-expert-witnesses-part-2)

### Inferences
- A "Part 35 mode" checklist the app can enforce: qualifications block; literature/data sources (e.g., Thatcham/Audatex/valuation guides, with versions and dates); a summary of instructions with the instruction letter attached (instructions are not privileged under 35.10(4) [verify]); facts within own knowledge marked separately from facts reported by others (e.g., a claimant's account versus the engineer's own inspection); who took the photos, who inspected, and when; range-of-opinion field (mandatory when a valuation or category is borderline); summary of conclusions; qualification field; duty declaration; the exact PD 35 statement of truth with the contempt sentence; signature and date.
- Track flag: on small claims, warn that permission is needed and fees are capped at £750. Add a "questions under 35.6" workflow with a 28-day timer.
- AI disclosure: given the Oct 2026 CJC recommendation, the app should log whether AI generated or influenced substantive content (e.g., AI damage detection or estimate lines) and offer an AI-use declaration and an audit trail, in case the revised guidance requires one.

## 4. Thatcham repair methods, BS 10125, ADAS calibration

### Takeaway
BS 10125 (current edition BS 10125:2022) replaced PAS 125. It underpins the BSI Kitemark for Vehicle Damage Repair that insurers and OEMs recognise. Thatcham's escribe platform is the main source of UK repair methods and times (Thatcham Time System since 1993). Insurers expect repairs to follow OEM or Thatcham methods. ADAS work is governed by Thatcham's 2020 Insurance Industry Requirements (IIR): if ADAS sensors, nearby parts or vehicle geometry are touched, consider inspection, realignment and calibration to OEM tolerances, with verifiable evidence of success. No "PAS 1886" ADAS standard could be found.

### Cited Findings
- PAS 125:2011 became BS 10125:2014 on 30 Nov 2014. PAS 125 certification ended from Dec 2015. The current standard is BS 10125:2022. — [BSI Kitemark BS10125 changes](https://www.bsigroup.com/Documents/PAS/PAS%20125/BSI-Kitemark-BS10125-Changes-UK-EN.pdf); [BSI BS 10125 page](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-IE/products-and-services/assessment-and-certification/product-testing-certification/deliver-safe-and-compliant-vehicle-repairs-to-bs-10125/); [Bodyshop mag comparison](https://www.bodyshopmag.com/?p=7938)
- The Kitemark requires a pre-licence audit. Capability tasks are coded (e.g., repair preparation, dent removal, plastic repair, refinishing). Repairers audit subcontractors every year. — [BSI](https://bsigroup.com/en-IN/Our-services/Product-certification/Industry-sector-schemes/Automotive-product-certification-and-Kitemark-schemes/bs-10125-bsi-kitemark-vehicle-damage-repair-automotive); [Toyota GB case study](https://www.bsigroup.com/Documents/Automotive/Toyota-GB-Casestudy-web.pdf)
- Thatcham Time System (1993) holds part/panel replace times built on researched repair methods. The data is in most estimating systems. escribe is the methods platform for engineers, VDAs and repairers, adding about 7,000 methods a month. Insurers say S and N repairs should follow OEM or Thatcham methods. — [Paint & Panel](https://www.paintandpanel.com.au/article/6D3499B0-3215-11DF-87AD005056B05D57); [Thatcham escribe 10 yrs](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/thatcham-research/pressreleases/moving-with-the-times-thatcham-researchs-escribe-celebrates-10-years-of-providing-vital-intelligence-to-the-repair-industry-3262521); [Thatcham headlamp repair](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/thatcham-research/pressreleases/thatcham-research-enables-sustainable-headlamp-repair-with-new-guidance-3256352)
- ADAS history: 2016 voluntary ADAS Glazing Code of Practice; 2019 guidance that calibration "must be performed" to OEM tolerances when ADAS sensors or nearby parts are in the repair specification (where no method exists, ask the OEM dealer network); July 2020 UK Insurance Industry Requirements (IIR) plus an implementation guide. — [CollisionWeek 2016](https://collisionweek.com/2016/07/29/thatcham-works-repair-chains-develop-best-practice-recalibrating-adas-systems-windshield-replacement/); [Thatcham press](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/uk/thatcham-research/pressreleases/adas-post-repair-calibration-to-vehicle-manufacturer-tolerances-is-essential-2870958); [Fleet News 2020](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/news/car-industry-news/2020/07/31/insurance-industry-requirements-established-for-adas-equipped-vehicles); [Thatcham IIR Guidance July 2020](https://www.thatcham.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/006-IIR-Guidance-July-2020.pdf)
- IIR: inspection, realignment and calibration "must be considered" whenever ADAS sensors, related parts or vehicle geometry are part of a repair. Static calibration uses specialist equipment and conditions; dynamic calibration means driving to the OEM method. Repairers assess for ADAS sensors and record the result. Equipment suppliers must provide verifiable evidence of successful calibration. Windscreen replacement with a screen-mounted camera always needs calibration (Thatcham, quoted 2025). — [HELLA-hosted IIR guidance](https://hella.com/partnerworld/assets/documents/ADAS_IIR_Guidance_July_2020.pdf); [National Windscreens 2025 ADAS report](https://www.nationalwindscreens.co.uk/downloads/National%20Windscreens%202025%20A4%20ADAS%20Report.pdf)
- BSI updated the automotive windscreen repair code of practice in March 2022 (per National Windscreens). — [National Windscreens 2025](https://www.nationalwindscreens.co.uk/downloads/National%20Windscreens%202025%20A4%20ADAS%20Report.pdf)
- IMI occupational standards (IMIVA02a/b 2025, IMILV19) cover static, dynamic and hybrid recalibration to OEM environmental specifications. — [IMIVA02a 2025](https://tide.theimi.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-11/IMIVA02a-2025.pdf); [UK Standards IMILV19](https://www.ukstandards.org.uk/en/nos-finder/IMILV19/diagnose-remove-replace-and-recalibrate-motor-vehicle-advanced-driver-assistance-system-components)

### Inferences
- App prompts: record the ADAS fit (via VIN/build data) and flag any repair line touching a sensor zone (bumper, grille, windscreen, mirrors, suspension/geometry) so calibration is added (static/dynamic/both, to OEM). Require a calibration certificate in post-repair or audit reports. Record the method source (OEM or Thatcham escribe, with reference and date) and the repairer's BS 10125 Kitemark status.
- Estimate validation: compare labour times with Thatcham/OEM times, and flag missing SRS or structural lines (the same rule as the IAEA exam).

### Gaps
- "PAS 1886" could not be identified. It may not exist under that number for ADAS; I found no BSI ADAS-calibration PAS.
- Whether Thatcham has issued a post-2020 ADAS code replacing the IIR is unconfirmed.

## 5. Economic repair / total loss, betterment, diminution

### Takeaway
There is no regulated total-loss threshold. Insurers set their own triggers, typically about 50–70% (some up to 75–80%) of pre-accident value (PAV). The decision weighs repair cost against PAV minus salvage, and is governed by policy wording. In third-party damages, the measure is diminution in value, normally proved by the reasonable cost of repair (Coles v Hetherton [2013] EWCA Civ 1704). Betterment and post-repair diminution are separate, fact-dependent issues with little UK motor-specific guidance found.

### Cited Findings
- Most UK insurers use a 60–70% threshold; others cite 50–60% or 75%; one insurer was quoted at 80%; one trade view is that about 60% should be the target. — [Raw2K](https://www.raw2k.co.uk/news/repair-cost-thresholds-when-insurance-writes-off-a-vehicle); [Insurance Times](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/whats-it-worth-to-you/1376699.article); [AXA](https://www.axa.co.uk/car-insurance/tips-and-guides/how-is-total-loss-calculated/)
- FOS: insurers may decline to write off even above 60–70% of market value, and the policy does not need repair cost to equal market value for a total loss. Policy wording and the insurer's valuation control the outcome. — [FOS DRN-5763454](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5763454.pdf); [FOS DRN-3674013](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-3674013.pdf)
- Coles v Hetherton: the loss is diminution in value, normally measured by the reasonable cost of repair (a question of fact). Reasonableness is judged from the claimant's own position, not the insurer's group procurement. The insurer's administrative/sundry costs are recoverable if the overall cost is reasonable. — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2013/12/coles-v-hetherton-ors); [judgment](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2013/1704); [DAC Beachcroft](https://www.dacbeachcroft.com/en/what-we-think/repairs-how-to-challenge-the-unreasonable)
- Betterment: in general UK common law (construction context), a deduction usually applies only where the claimant chose a higher standard. It is usually not made where the claimant had no reasonable choice. — [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/uk/insights/articles/construction-damages-rectification-defects-issues-betterment)
- FOS: restoring a car to its visual pre-accident state is not betterment. Post-repair loss of market value can be excluded from policy cover. Pre-accident damage and wear should be noted. — [FOS DRN-4470683](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4470683.pdf); [FOS DRN-4568894](https://financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4568894.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app should compute and show repair cost as a percentage of PAV, and (repair cost + hire/storage exposure) against (PAV − salvage). It should flag a configurable insurer threshold (default band 50–70%) and record the source of the PAV (named guide, date, mileage/condition adjustments, comparables). A range of values should be stated in Part 35 mode.
- Prompts: pre-existing/unrelated damage, wear items and corrosion (betterment candidates, with a reason); whether diminution after repair is claimed (needs separate evidence).

### Gaps
- No authoritative UK motor guidance on betterment percentages or diminution-after-repair method was found.

## 6. Challenges to engineer reports; desktop/AI assessment

### Takeaway
Engineer reports in credit hire and total-loss claims fail on independence, on inconsistency between notes, photos and report, on speculative findings, and on desktop reports lacking coverage (e.g., no underside photos). Insurers use AI tools at scale (Tractable with Ageas, Aviva and an Admiral subsidiary; Solera Qapter photo estimating), mainly for non-structural or desktop work with human review. No IAEA guidance on image-only assessment was found. The Oct 2026 CJC report is the first formal move toward AI disclosure in expert reports.

### Cited Findings
- Hodroje v Storm of London & Ageas: the engineer had only handwritten notes and photos at trial, the report differed from the notes, photos showed no repairs, and the judge called the findings "speculative assertions". The PAV failed and with it the hire and recovery/storage claims. The report had declared the car an undriveable write-off (PAV less salvage £2,394) on trivial damage, and the disclosed instructions did not support the stated boot-lid defect. — [Weightmans](https://www.weightmans.com/media-centre/news/when-expert-engineering-evidence-in-a-vehicle-damage-and-credit-hire-claim-is-not-what-it-seems/)
- A Hastings/H-F case: the judge gave "very little weight" to a Part 35 engineer, and a £50k claim was dismissed. — [H-F, "Engineered to fail"](https://www.h-f.co.uk/news/engineered-to-fail-50k-claim-dismissed/)
- Wiltshire v Aioi Nissay Dowa [2025] EWCC 13: the expert had "little or no regard" to Part 35; the solicitors' directors were also directors of the credit hire company; hire was claimed for two vehicles when one was supplied. (Slide summary only.) — [Deka Chambers slides, Oct 2025](https://www.dekachambers.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Civil-Fraud-Credit-Update-slide-16.10.2025-2.pdf); [judgment](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewcc/2025/13)
- Independence test: would the expert give the same opinion if instructed by the other side? Courts will not readily exclude evidence without clear impropriety (Blackpool BC v Volkerfitzpatrick). — [Fenwick Elliott](https://www.fenwickelliott.com/knowledge-hub/insight/issue-74/); [Blackpool v Volkerfitzpatrick](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2020/387)
- Desktop/photo-only lines of attack (Irish source, by analogy): no underside or suspension photos, missing inputs, request for the full file (Audatex printouts, photo sets, calculation sheets). Unreferenced or undated photos are easy to dispute. Photos in bundles may be small or black and white. — [Irish PI site 2026](https://www.personalinjurysolicitorsdublin.info/car-accident-claims/evidence/insurer-engineer-report-disputes/); [Freedom Assessors](https://www.freedomassessors.co.uk/engineering-evidence-is-key-to-defending-credit-hire-claims/)
- AI: Ageas was the first UK insurer to use Tractable for end-to-end assessments and estimates (2020). Aviva/Tractable (2022) aims to "assist and guide engineers" in remote assessment. An Admiral subsidiary uses Tractable. Tractable says its system can handle non-structural claims with no human. Solera Qapter produces photo-based preliminary estimates with human-in-the-loop review, and Qapter Direct Dispatch was announced in August 2026 (US-focused). — [Tractable/Ageas](https://tractable.ai/ageas-is-first-uk-insurer-to-use-ai-to-create-end-to-end-car-damage-assessments-and-estimates/); [Insurance Times Aviva](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/aviva-inks-deal-with-tractable-to-overhaul-uk-motor-claims-accuracy/1440996.article); [Insurance Times Admiral](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/admiral-subsidiary-using-tractables-ai-solution/1434760.article); [Insurance Times Tractable](https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/ageas-ai-partner-tractable-takes-its-claims-product-to-next-level/1425391.article); [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2020/07/10/solera-new-version-of-qapter-allows-ai-photo-estimating/); [Bodyshop mag Solera](https://bodyshopmag.com/?p=34375); [Qapter Direct Dispatch](https://pr.timesofsandiego.com/article/Solera-Launches-Qapterr-Direct-Dispatch-the-First-Solution-to-Deliver-Nationwide-Estimating-Access-for-Insurers/6a75cbf12e905784f149a529)
- CJC (Oct 2026): proposed AI declaration/guidance for experts where AI generates or influences substantive content. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/05/cjc-final-report-on-ai-and-court-documents-declarations-for-witnesses-and-expert-102o8fa)

### Inferences
- Robustness checks the app can run: an inspection-type field (physical / desktop / video), and if desktop, a limitations statement plus a coverage checklist (four corners, close-ups, underside, interior/airbags, odometer, VIN plate, tyres); photo metadata (who, when, EXIF/geo) and photo references cited in the text; contemporaneous notes reconciled with the final report (the Hodroje failure); instructions attached, with a conflict-of-interest declaration (relationship to CHO, solicitor, repairer, insurer; fee basis); a driveability/roadworthiness finding tied to specific defects with photos (it drives hire-period claims); and an AI-use log.

### Gaps
- No IAEA, IMI or ABI guidance on remote/image-only assessment was found. No English judgment directly ruling on photo-only engineer reports was found.
- Solera Qapter's 2025–26 UK insurer use was not confirmed.
